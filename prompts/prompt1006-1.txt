3. CORE FAILURE DIAGNOSIS
==================================================

Audit three possible reasons for Gate 2 failure:

A. RESPONSE-MIXING FAILURE

YCEO SUHI is:

urban LST - rural-reference LST

Therefore R combines:

- urban-side thermal state;
- rural/background thermal state;
- rural vegetation/aridity/topography;
- cluster-boundary/reference construction.

History may influence the urban side without predictably influencing
the urban-minus-rural contrast.

B. HISTORY-REPRESENTATION FAILURE

Current H captures only aggregate built-up-area trajectory.

It does not directly capture:

- morphological evolution;
- verticalization;
- compactness;
- street/building geometry;
- material change;
- green-infrastructure evolution;
- fragmentation/polycentricity;
- land-cover transition sequence.

C. SCALE / ONTOLOGY FAILURE

YCEO is a coarse urban-agglomeration product with fixed Natural-Earth-derived
cluster polygons, while GHSL history is defined using a different urban ontology.

Even after conservative matching, historical information may be attenuated
by aggregation and footprint mismatch.

Assess each explanation using evidence already generated.

Do NOT use post-hoc significance as the primary criterion.

5. CANDIDATE PIVOT HYPOTHESES
==================================================

Evaluate at least the following four candidate scientific pivots.

--------------------------------------------------
PIVOT A — URBAN-SIDE THERMAL RESPONSE
--------------------------------------------------

Question:

Does developmental history predict urban-side thermal state
after controlling for regional/background climate,
even though it does not predict the urban-rural SUHI contrast?

Potential response concepts:

- urban mean LST;
- urban nighttime LST;
- urban thermal anomaly relative to broad climate rather than local rural pixels;
- residualized urban LST after temperature/climate controls;
- urban cooling/heating persistence.

Audit whether a global city/agglomeration-level pre-aggregated product exists.

Strong preference:

city/cluster-level table or vector.

Avoid global raw MODIS raster processing unless no scientifically valid
aggregated alternative exists.

--------------------------------------------------
PIVOT B — MORPHOLOGICAL HISTORY
--------------------------------------------------

Question:

Does historical evolution of urban morphology contain information
that aggregate built-up-area trajectory misses?

Candidate H concepts:

- built-volume trajectory;
- built-height / verticalization trajectory;
- density trajectory;
- compactness evolution;
- fragmentation / polycentricity evolution;
- expansion mode:
  infill vs edge expansion vs leapfrog;
- radial expansion shape.

Audit whether GHSL or another globally consistent source can support
a LOW-VOLUME city-level representation.

Do NOT propose global pixel-scale processing without first proving necessity.

--------------------------------------------------
PIVOT C — DYNAMIC-BOUNDARY REPLICATION
--------------------------------------------------

Question:

Was the positive dynamic-MTUC result a real signal or a subset/specification artifact?

Design an independent replication strategy.

Requirements:

- pre-registered;
- no reuse of the exact same significance path as evidence;
- same-sample fixed-vs-dynamic comparison;
- independent outcome or time period preferred;
- explicit null / pseudo-H controls.

--------------------------------------------------
PIVOT D — RESPONSE-SPECIFIC HISTORY
--------------------------------------------------

Question:

Is thermal SUHI simply the wrong environmental response for the history mechanism,
while another response has stronger path dependence?

Candidate response families may include:

- vegetation/greenness resilience;
- surface moisture / evapotranspiration;
- urban ecological productivity;
- flood/runoff proxy;
- air quality if globally consistent;
- land-surface thermal variability rather than mean SUHI.

Do NOT choose a response because it gives a positive result.

Assess mechanistic plausibility, temporal ordering, global coverage,
data volume, circularity risk, and confounding.
