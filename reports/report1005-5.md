**This execution is a robustness gate, not Gate 2. No environmental

## 1. Executive Summary

Execution 1005-4's Gate 1 PASS was based on the main GHS-UCDB product,
whose historical attributes are aggregated within a boundary fixed at
the 2025 reference epoch — raising the concern that apparent historical
separability could be an artifact of that fixed-boundary conditioning.
This execution re-tests the core Gate 1 finding using the official
GHS_UCDB_MTUC_GLOBE_R2024A product, whose boundaries are recalculated
independently for every epoch.

**Headline findings:**

1. No official crosswalk, shared identifier, or lineage field links
   UCDB and MTUC. A deterministic name+country correspondence, corroborated
   by independent 2020 population/built-up agreement, was constructed and
   further restricted to MTUC entities with complete (non-missing)
   1975–2020 history, yielding a conservative **matched sample of
   N=4,739 cities** (of 11,422 UCDB cities).
2. **H_fixed and H_dynamic agree strongly** (mean per-epoch Pearson
   r=0.89, ranging 0.94 at 1975 down to 0.75 at 2015) — the two boundary
   definitions measure substantially the same underlying historical
   signal.
3. **The core Gate 1 phenomenon does not disappear under the dynamic
   boundary — if anything, two of three core metrics show it is equally
   or more pronounced:** reconstruction R² is *lower* under H_dynamic
   (0.158 vs. 0.320 for H_fixed on the identical matched sample — i.e.
   history is *harder*, not easier, to predict from present state/context
   under the dynamic boundary), and the divergent-twin rate is
   *slightly higher* under H_dynamic (18.7% vs. 16.7%). The one metric
   that shows attenuation is the permutation effect size (23.6% dynamic
   vs. 31.3% fixed) — reduced but still clearly non-trivial.
4. This pattern — stable or strengthened on two of three core tests,
   moderately attenuated on the third — **was consistent across all
   9 sensitivity variants tested** (smallest-city removal, QC-jump
   removal, 5 regions, alternate endpoint T=2015, alternate k=3/10).
5. **GATE 1B = MIXED.** Not PASS, because the R² comparison technically
   exceeds the pre-registered 0.15 tolerance; not FAIL, because the
   *direction* of that change (more, not less, unexplained historical
   variation under the more rigorous dynamic boundary) is the opposite
   of what would indicate the original Gate 1 PASS was a fixed-boundary
   artifact. **There is no evidence that execution 1005-4's Gate 1 PASS
   was primarily generated by fixed-boundary conditioning.**

No environmental response variable was used, no causal claim is made,
and no 1005-1 through 1005-4 artifact was modified.

## 4. UCDB↔MTUC Correspondence Method

**No official crosswalk field, documented shared identifier, or
documented parent/child lineage field was found** in either product's
schema or PDF data dictionary (both searched directly, not assumed).
Per the prompt's preference order, this falls to criterion 4:
**deterministic attribute correspondence with transparent, documented
criteria** — specifically:

1. Normalize and join on (city name, country), both generated by the
   same geocoding algorithm in both products.
2. Keep only **one-to-one** keys (both sides unique) — **10,744** of
   11,422 UCDB cities (94.1%).
3. **Numeric corroboration:** require 2020 population AND built-up-area
   ratios (UCDB/MTUC) both within [0.2, 5.0] — a generous but
   non-trivial independent check. 10,263 of 10,744 pass (**481 rejected**
   as likely false-positive name collisions or substantial boundary
   divergence at 2020).
4. **Complete-history restriction:** further require the MTUC record's
   `GC_UCB_YOB_2025 ≤ 1975` (no missing epochs across 1975–2020, no
   interpolation) and the UCDB record's `GC_UCB_YOB_2025 ≤ 2020`
   (consistent with 1005-4's own primary-sample exclusion rule).

**Result:** N_UCDB=11,422; N_MTUC=11,686; N_matched (one-to-one
name+country)=10,744; N_ambiguous (many-sided keys)=165; N_UCDB_unmatched
=319... *(see note below)*; N_MTUC_unmatched=268; N numeric-corroboration
failures=481; **N_core_matched_sample=4,739**.

Full per-pair audit (every accepted/rejected mapping, with reasons):
`results/1005-5/ucdb_mtuc_crosswalk.csv`.

**This is a deliberately conservative sample, not a maximized one** —
no fuzzy matching was performed, and the complete-history requirement
alone removes a majority of the numerically-corroborated one-to-one
matches. The consequence (disclosed in full in Section 15) is a sample
systematically skewed toward **older, already-established-by-1975**
cities.

## 6. H_fixed Definition

Identical in form to execution 1005-4's primary H: h_i(t) =
`GH_BUS_TOT_i(t)` / `GH_BUS_TOT_i(2020)` for t ∈ {1975,...,2015} (9
epochs), computed on the matched sample's UCDB (fixed-boundary) records.

## 7. H_dynamic Definition

h_i(t) = `MT_BUS_TOT_i(t)` / `MT_BUS_TOT_i(2020)` for the same 9 epochs,
computed on the matched sample's MTUC (time-varying-boundary) records.
**B_dynamic(2020) has a different scientific meaning from B_fixed(2020):**
B_fixed(2020) is the built-up surface within the city's *fixed, 2025-
delineated* boundary, summed using 2020 imagery; B_dynamic(2020) is the
built-up surface within the boundary *as independently delineated using
2020's own population distribution*. The two can differ even at the
shared endpoint, which is exactly why the numeric corroboration check in
Section 4 uses a generous [0.2, 5.0] tolerance rather than requiring
near-exact agreement, and why `D_boundary` (Section 8) is non-zero even
though both trajectories are individually self-normalized to 1.0 at
their own endpoint.

## 8. City-Level Boundary Agreement

Full table: `results/1005-5/boundary_history_agreement.csv`. Per-epoch
Pearson correlation between H_fixed and H_dynamic: **0.943 (1975) →
0.907 (2000) → 0.752 (2015)** — strong agreement throughout, *declining*
as the epoch approaches the endpoint (not improving, as might be
naively expected). Spearman rank correlations are consistently slightly
higher than Pearson at every epoch, indicating the relationship is
monotonic but not perfectly linear. Descriptor agreement (early/mid/
recent-expansion fractions) mirrors the corresponding epoch correlations
exactly (by construction, since these descriptors are direct functions
of specific epochs).

**D_boundary** (per-city Euclidean distance between the two 9-dimensional
trajectories) breakdown (`results/1005-5/boundary_sensitivity_breakdown.csv`):

- **By size:** boundary sensitivity *increases* with city size (small
  cities: mean D_boundary=0.139; medium: 0.175; large: 0.218) — larger
  cities' boundaries are more sensitive to the fixed-vs-dynamic choice.
- **By region:** North America (0.258) and Oceania (0.291, n=16, too
  small to be reliable) show the highest sensitivity; Africa, Asia,
  Europe, and South America cluster around 0.16–0.18.
- **By recency:** **cannot be assessed within this sample** — zero
  cities in the core matched sample have UCDB YOB>1990, because the
  complete-MTUC-history requirement (Section 4) structurally excludes
  essentially all recently-urbanized cities. This is an important,
  disclosed limitation, not a null finding (Section 15).
- **By expansion speed:** rapidly-expanding cities (low h_fixed(1975))
  show slightly *lower* D_boundary (0.166) than slowly-expanding ones
  (0.188) — a modest, not dramatic, difference.

## 9. Reconstruction Comparison

Full table: `results/1005-5/reconstruction_boundary_comparison.csv`
(leave-region-out, random forest, S3+C, identical present-state/context
specification for both H definitions, computed on the identical matched
sample).

| | R² (mean across 9 epochs) | Aggregate trajectory RMSE |
|---|---|---|
| **H_fixed** (matched sample) | **0.320** | 0.319 |
| **H_dynamic** (matched sample) | **0.158** | 0.388 |
| *1005-4 H_fixed, full sample (reference)* | *0.226* | *n/a (different metric scale)* |

**R²_dynamic is lower than R²_fixed at every single one of the 9
epochs**, not just on average — including a *negative* R² at 2015 under
H_dynamic (worse than a mean predictor). The matched-sample R²_fixed
(0.320) is itself notably *higher* than 1005-4's full-sample figure
(0.226), consistent with the matched sample's skew toward older,
more-established cities (Section 15) having more stable, predictable
trajectories. **The change from fixed to dynamic is moderate-to-large
in absolute R² terms (−0.162) but is directionally reassuring, not
alarming: it means the dynamic (arguably more correct) boundary shows
MORE unresolved historical variation, not less** — the opposite of what
would be expected if 1005-4's PASS were an artifact of the fixed
boundary artificially suppressing predictability.

## 10. Twin Comparison

Full table: `results/1005-5/twin_boundary_comparison.csv`. Using
separately-estimated (within-definition) quartile thresholds, k=5:

| | Divergent-twin rate |
|---|---|
| **H_fixed** (matched sample) | **16.7%** |
| **H_dynamic** (matched sample) | **18.7%** |
| *1005-4 H_fixed, full sample (reference)* | *18.3%* |

The matched-sample H_fixed rate (16.7%) is close to 1005-4's full-sample
reference (18.3%), and **H_dynamic's rate is not lower — it is slightly
higher**. Using the alternative approach (D_H threshold held fixed at
H_fixed's own cutoff, applied to both definitions), H_dynamic's rate
rises further to **28.2%**, because H_dynamic trajectories carry more
absolute variability overall (consistent with the lower R² in Section 9)
— both threshold approaches point the same direction: **the divergent-
twin phenomenon does not shrink under the dynamic boundary.**

## 13. Evidence That Gate 1 Survives Boundary Correction

1. Divergent-twin rate under H_dynamic (18.7%) remains non-trivial and
   is *not lower* than H_fixed's matched-sample rate (16.7%).
2. Permutation relative effect size under H_dynamic (23.6%) remains
   substantial (>10%) and is the same order of magnitude as H_fixed's
   matched-sample effect (31.3%).
3. Mean per-epoch correlation between H_fixed and H_dynamic is high
   (0.894) — the two boundary definitions measure substantially the
   same underlying signal, not unrelated quantities.
4. The pattern is stable across all 9 sensitivity variants tested
   (Section 12) — not an artifact of one region, one size class, one QC
   choice, one endpoint year, or one k value.
5. Where reconstruction R² changes, it changes in the *reassuring*
   direction (dynamic boundary shows *more*, not less, unexplained
   historical variation) — directly contrary to what a "fixed-boundary-
   artifact" explanation of 1005-4's PASS would predict.

## 14. Evidence That Gate 1 Does NOT Survive Boundary Correction

1. Reconstruction R² drops materially in absolute terms from 0.320
   (H_fixed, matched) to 0.158 (H_dynamic, matched) — a −0.162 change,
   exceeding this report's pre-set 0.15 "trivial change" tolerance.
2. The permutation relative effect size is consistently and
   non-negligibly lower under H_dynamic (23.6% vs. 31.3%, roughly a
   25% relative reduction) across every sensitivity variant — a real,
   reproducible attenuation, even though it does not approach zero.
3. The matched sample itself (Section 15) is a systematically
   non-representative subset of the full UCDB population (older,
   already-urban-by-1975 cities only), so neither the fixed nor the
   dynamic matched-sample figures can be assumed to generalize to the
   full, more recently-urbanizing global population without further
   work.

## 15. Scientific Limitations

1. **No official identity crosswalk exists.** The name+country
   correspondence, while corroborated numerically, is an attribute-level
   construction, not an authoritative linkage; 481 initially-matched
   pairs failed numeric corroboration and were excluded, and it is
   possible a small number of true correspondences were missed or a
   small number of false correspondences were retained despite
   corroboration.
2. **The complete-history requirement makes the core matched sample
   systematically non-representative.** By construction, it contains
   *zero* cities with UCDB YOB>1990 — i.e., it cannot speak to whether
   boundary sensitivity differs for recently-urbanized cities (one of
   the four diagnostic questions explicitly requested in Section 6 of
   the governing prompt). This is reported as a limitation, not
   papered over with an unjustified claim either way.
3. **No split/merge lineage field exists in either product**; this
   execution cannot rule out that some one-to-one name+country matches
   actually represent entities that underwent an undocumented split or
   merge between the two delineation schemes, though the tight numeric
   corroboration (median population ratio ≈ 1.000003 among accepted
   matches) makes large undetected discrepancies unlikely.
4. **B_dynamic(2020) and B_fixed(2020) are not the same physical
   quantity** (Section 7); all comparisons in this report are therefore
   comparisons of *self-normalized trajectory shape*, not of absolute
   built-up levels.
5. **Oceania (n=16) is too small** for any region-specific conclusion
   (correctly skipped in the sensitivity table, not force-computed).
6. This remains a **Gate 1B robustness check only** — it does not test,
   and cannot support any claim about, history's relationship to any
   environmental response.

## 16. Gate 1B Decision

**GATE 1B = MIXED.** Full machine-readable decision:
`results/1005-5/gate1b_decision.json`. Three pieces of converging
evidence support survival (Section 13); the R² and effect-size
attenuation (Section 14, items 1–2) prevent an unqualified PASS; no
evidence supports FAIL (the phenomenon never disappears, reverses, or
collapses toward the null in any test or sensitivity variant) and the
correspondence/comparison was rigorous enough to decide (ruling out
UNRESOLVED). **There is no evidence that execution 1005-4's Gate 1 PASS
was primarily generated by fixed-boundary conditioning** — if anything,
the more rigorous dynamic-boundary measure shows *more* unresolved
historical variation on two of three core tests, with the third
(permutation effect size) showing real but non-collapsing attenuation.
Per the governing prompt, MIXED means boundary definition must become a
central, explicit part of any future scientific design (not that the
phenomenon is in doubt).

## 19. Questions for PI Review

**Q1. Can MTUC be interpreted as a scientifically valid time-varying-
boundary longitudinal urban dataset?**
Yes, with an important structural caveat: official documentation
confirms genuinely per-epoch dynamic boundaries (not merely re-labeled
fixed data), but the resulting missingness (56.5% at 1975) is
structural (pre-qualification absence), requiring the complete-history
restriction in Section 4 rather than any interpolation.

**Q2. How many UCDB cities can be conservatively compared with MTUC?**
4,739 (41.5% of the 11,422 UCDB cities), after name+country matching,
numeric corroboration, and the complete-1975–2020-history requirement.

**Q3. Are unmatched/ambiguous cities systematically different?**
Not formally tested (out of scope for this execution's core tests), but
by construction, excluded cities include essentially all recently-
urbanized cities (UCDB YOB>1990) and 481 numeric-corroboration
failures — both groups are plausibly systematically different
(newer/smaller/more volatile), a disclosed limitation (Section 15).

**Q4. How strongly do H_fixed and H_dynamic agree?**
Strongly: mean per-epoch Pearson r=0.894, ranging 0.943 (1975) to 0.752
(2015).

**Q5. Which types of cities are most boundary-sensitive?**
Larger cities (mean D_boundary rises from 0.139 for small to 0.218 for
large) and, with low reliability given small n, North America/Oceania.
Recency could not be assessed (Section 15).

**Q6. On the exact same matched sample, what is R²_fixed?**
0.320.

**Q7. On the exact same matched sample, what is R²_dynamic?**
0.158.

**Q8. Does the reconstruction conclusion materially change?**
The magnitude changes (−0.162), but the *direction* does not: H remains
far from well-reconstructed from S,C under either boundary definition,
and the dynamic boundary shows *more*, not less, unresolved variation.

**Q9. What is the matched-sample divergent-twin rate under H_fixed?**
16.7%.

**Q10. What is it under H_dynamic?**
18.7% (separately-estimated thresholds); 28.2% under a threshold held
fixed from H_fixed.

**Q11. Does the twin phenomenon survive k=3,5,10?**
Yes — divergent-twin rate under H_dynamic remains at or above H_fixed's
at every k tested (Section 12).

**Q12. How does the constrained-null effect size change under dynamic
boundaries?**
It decreases from 31.3% (fixed) to 23.6% (dynamic) — a real,
consistent ~25% relative reduction, but remains clearly non-trivial.

**Q13. Does the phenomenon survive T=2015?**
Yes — the T=2015 sensitivity variant shows divergent-twin rates (16.0%
fixed, 18.1% dynamic) and effect sizes (31.8% fixed, 24.1% dynamic)
essentially unchanged from the T=2020 baseline.

**Q14. Is there evidence that the 1005-4 Gate 1 PASS was primarily an
artifact of fixed 2025 boundaries?**
**No.** If fixed-boundary conditioning were primarily responsible,
switching to the dynamic boundary should have made H *more* predictable
from S,C and the divergent-twin/effect-size signals should have
collapsed. Instead, reconstruction R² *decreased* and the divergent-twin
rate did not decrease — the opposite of that prediction. The one
genuine attenuation (permutation effect size) is real but partial, not
a collapse.

**Q15. What is the strongest evidence AGAINST robustness?**
The absolute R² drop (0.320→0.158) and the consistent ~25% relative
reduction in permutation effect size, both reproducible across every
sensitivity variant tested.

**Q16. What is the strongest evidence FOR robustness?**
